How cost is measured

Four token classes, a price table, and the SDK's own figure whenever it reports one.

Every model call writes a usage row with provider, model, input, output, cache-write and cache-read tokens, the cost and the stage it served. The cost comes from the provider SDK when it reports one, otherwise from the price table. Interactive developer sessions are captured by a hook into the same ledger, so human-in-the-loop work is not invisible.

86 cached tokens are read for every token written. Cache reads are priced at a tenth of fresh input, so the cost of a change is mostly the cost of re-reading the repository.

Audit the meter

The report that produced these figures also audits its own meter. We bring that audit to the table.

A cost figure is only as good as the ledger behind it. The economics report checks the ledger against a second one and reports the disagreement, so the headline arrives with its error bars.

Two ledgers disagree by six percent. The usage rows hold 11.983 billion tokens and $9,486; the stage-event ledger holds 11.285 billion and $8,865. The event ledger understates by 697.7 million tokens and $620.87, because usage is written after a stage completes and provider failures, timeouts, cancellations and process deaths can occur first.

Three coverage gaps, quantified. 405 ordinary jobs have no usage row, 24 of them delivered. 93.8% of historical usage rows lack model metadata, so the Codex-versus-Claude split cannot be quoted. 3,635 rows report zero cost, most of them unpriced models before the price table was completed.

What that changes

The headline is a recorded minimum, not an estimate. The report's recommendation, one durable stage-attempt ledger opened before every provider call and finalised on every exit path, reconciled against provider logs, is on the roadmap. Project comparisons reflect different models, dates and workloads as well as engineering efficiency, and are presented that way.
